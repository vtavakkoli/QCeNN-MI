\subsection{Matched Time-Varying Comparison Against Restarted and One-Shot Filtering}
\label{sec:matched_temporal_comparison}

The relevance of recurrent channels is not established by comparing their
macro-update counts with an unrelated static solver. We therefore isolate
the effect of retaining a physical data state across changes of the matrix
and right-hand side. At each snapshot $t$ we form the augmented residual
$G_t$ with a common certified lower bound $g$ on nonzero eigenvalues and a
bounded signed response $B_{d,t}=r_d(G_t)$. The channel is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_{d,t}(\rho)=B_{d,t}\rho B_{d,t}^{\dagger}
 +\operatorname{Tr}[(I-B_{d,t}^{\dagger}B_{d,t})\rho]R,
\end{equation}
where $R=|r\rangle\langle r|$ is known independently of the solution.

\emph{Resident-state recurrence} carries $\rho_{t-1}^{\mathrm{resident}}$
into the next snapshot and applies $K$ copies of the new channel:
$\rho_t^{\mathrm{resident}}=\mathcal{E}_{d,t}^{K}
(\rho_{t-1}^{\mathrm{resident}})$. \emph{Restarted recurrence} uses the
same map, degree, matrix, and number of oracle queries, but initializes
from $R$ at each snapshot:
$\rho_t^{\mathrm{fresh}}=\mathcal{E}_{d,t}^{K}(R)$.
The latter is a strict same-output-model, same-oracle-budget comparator.
To benchmark an established nonrecurrent alternative, \emph{direct
one-shot filtering} prepares
\begin{equation}
\rho_t^{\mathrm{direct}}
=\frac{B_{s,t}R B_{s,t}^{\dagger}}{p_t},\qquad
p_t=\operatorname{Tr}(B_{s,t}^{\dagger}B_{s,t}R),
\end{equation}
conditioning on a successful QSP signal outcome. The one-shot degree
$s=\lfloor Q/2\rfloor$ spends at most $Q$ input-unitary calls per attempt.
The recurrent macro degree $d$ uses $2d$ calls, and
$K=\lfloor Q/(2d)\rfloor$. Although all methods have the same single-attempt
per-snapshot query ceiling, accepted direct preparations cost an expected
$2s/p_t$ calls before measurements. The postselected and unconditional
state-output tasks are thus not assumed interchangeable.

The controlled schedules rotate right singular vectors while keeping a
specified condition number; the right-hand sides undergo gradual or
abrupt changes. The original physical density is preserved in the
resident method---a spectral basis transformation in the classical
simulator is not a quantum reset or classical correction. At every
snapshot we score a common relative residual
$\|M_t\widehat{x}_t-b_t\|_2/\|b_t\|_2$ and conditional solution infidelity
using reference-coherence readout. Classical solutions are only scoring
references and never appear in an update operator. We report the paired
number of passing snapshots, the per-snapshot failure trajectories,
query charges, direct-filter acceptance probability, and the expected
calls per accepted preparation. We retain ties and regressions.

Finite phase synthesis is audited separately: the QSP success block
is compared with the exact signed polynomial, both the empirical
Chebyshev coefficient residual and kernel leakage are retained, and
logical query-coupled depolarization is identified as a phenomenological
model rather than calibrated native-gate noise. The numerical results
establish the investigated operating region, not a universal recurrent
advantage. Dense spectral compilation, state preparation, repeated
shots, and readout remain limitations of the end-to-end cost model.
